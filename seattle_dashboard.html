Real public data: Seattle Fire 911 incidents, 351,099 records, Jan 2024 to Nov 2026

The problem: emergency call volume is not flat, but staffing usually is. This analysis uses 351,099 real Seattle Fire 911 incidents (2024-01-01 to 2026-11-24) to show how demand moves hour by hour and what that does to staffing.

What this means

  • Six of the ten biggest days fall in one week (12 to 17 Jan 2024) and are driven by "Water Job Minor" incidents on top of normal aid calls. The data does not state a cause; a cold snap is a plausible explanation, not a proven one.
  • Spikes are rare but need a plan. A surge roster or overflow agreement matters more than a bigger base team.

What this means

  • Weekday barely changes the load (Sat and Sun are about 3 to 5% lower). Plan by hour first.
  • Yearly volume fell about 8% from 2024 to 2025 and a little more in 2026. A fixed profile goes stale, so plans must roll forward.
  • Data gaps: 2024-09-19 (92 incidents), 2024-03-09 (119) and 2026-07-25 (191) look like feed outages, kept in the data and noted here.

Know the floor

At about 14 incidents an hour, random variation alone gives roughly 21% error. Chasing 10% hourly accuracy is wasted effort.

Method

Use the matched roster

3 agents overnight, 4 in the day, 5 at peaks. That frees about 215 agent-hours a week against flat-to-peak.

Keep a surge plan

Rare spike days reached 2x normal. Plan overflow, not permanent headcount.

Method and limits: incident records only (no call times, answer times, abandons or rosters).
